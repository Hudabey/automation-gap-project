1a  Separately excited DC motor  (V = 220 V, Ra = 0.5 ohm, k = 1.4 N·m/A)
    no-load speed      w0 = V/k            =   157.1 rad/s =   1501 rpm
    rated torque       T  = k*I (I = 30 A) =    42.0 N·m
    rated speed        w  = (V - I*Ra)/k   =   146.4 rad/s =   1398 rpm
    speed drop at rated load               =   6.8 %
    stall torque       T  = k*V/Ra         =     616 N·m  (15x rated -> needs current limiting)
    efficiency at rated point (copper loss only) = 93.2 %

1b  Induction motor  (4-pole, 400 V star, 50 Hz, R1 0.9, X1 2.4, R2' 0.95, X2' 2.4, Xm 50 ohm/phase)
    synchronous speed  ns = 120 f / p       =   1500 rpm
    rated point (5.5 kW): slip =  4.2 %, speed =  1438 rpm, torque =  36.4 N·m
    starting torque  (s = 1)                =  34.9 N·m  (1.0x rated)
    pull-out torque  (s = 0.199)           =  82.9 N·m  (2.3x rated)
    check: s_max = R2'/|Zth + jX2'|         = 0.199
    starting current (s = 1) =   45 A per phase  -> why star-delta or soft starters are used

1c  Inverter drive, constant V/f (V/f = 231/50 = 4.62 V/Hz per phase)
      f (Hz)   V (V)   ns (rpm)   pull-out, plain V/f   pull-out, with boost
         50    230.9      1500      82.9 N·m              82.9 N·m
         35    161.7      1050      76.8 N·m              80.2 N·m
         20     92.4       600      63.9 N·m              73.9 N·m
         10     46.2       300      43.7 N·m              62.9 N·m
          5     23.1       150      25.1 N·m              52.7 N·m
    Constant V/f keeps the flux, and so the torque curve shape, nearly constant while ns scales with f.
    At low f the stator resistance drop takes a larger share of V, flux falls, and pull-out torque drops;
    the boost adds voltage at low f to restore it.

1d  Conveyor drive for PLC Task 3 (assumed: 60 kg moving mass, mu 0.3, 0.25 m/s belt, 0.10 m drum, 0.5 s ramp)
    friction force        = mu*m*g            =  176.6 N
    acceleration force    = m*v/t             =   30.0 N
    drum torque           = F*d/2             =  10.33 N·m at   48 rpm
    gear ratio (motor about 1425 rpm)          =  29.8 : 1
    motor torque          = T_drum/(i*eta)    =  0.385 N·m
    steady shaft power    = F_fric*v/eta      =   49.1 W  -> a 0.12 kW 4-pole motor with a ~30:1 gearbox
    Drive choice: the cycle needs start/stop and two speeds at most, so a direct-on-line contactor
    (as in the PLC exercise, output M1) is enough. A small inverter would add soft ramps (protects the
    boards on the belt) and positioning at sensor B2, and is the better choice if parts slip at start.
